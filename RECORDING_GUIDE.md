# Recording Guide: "One Day, I..."

Casey — here's how to get a clean vocal recording that I can actually work with for the cover project.

## Why Re-Record?

The original 11-second clip is beautiful, but the vocals sit at **-74 dB** — that's below the noise floor of the recording. Every separation model I've tried (6 different Demucs architectures, spectral filtering, AI enhancement) agrees: the voice and the guitar are fused at the frequency level. No algorithm currently exists that can pull them apart cleanly.

The good news: a 30-second re-recording on your phone will give me everything I need.

## What You Need

- **Your phone** (any smartphone from the last 5 years is fine)
- A quiet room (not echoey — a bedroom with a closed door works great)
- Your guitar (if you want to play and sing simultaneously)

## How To Record

### Option A: Vocals Only (Best for Covers)
1. Open your phone's voice recorder app
2. Hold the phone **6-8 inches from your mouth** (about a hand's width)
3. Sing the lyrics. Don't worry about perfection — feel is what matters
4. Record at least **30 seconds** (one verse + chorus is plenty)
5. You can hum the melody if you forget the words — I can work with that too

### Option B: Voice + Guitar Together
1. Same setup, but position the phone **between your mouth and the guitar**, about 2 feet from each
2. Play and sing normally
3. The closer the mic is to your voice, the better the separation will work
4. **Don't use an external mic placed far away** — phone proximity is key

### Option C: Two Takes
1. First: record yourself singing with the guitar in your hands (for timing)
2. Second: record just your voice, singing along to the first take through headphones
3. Send me both files

## What I Need

- **Format:** Any audio format (.m4a, .wav, .mp3 — your phone's default is fine)
- **Length:** 30-60 seconds minimum (one verse and chorus, or the whole song if you want)
- **Quality:** As long as your voice is clearly audible above the guitar, I can work with it

## What I'll Do With It

1. **Separate** your vocals from guitar cleanly
2. **Extract** your exact melody as MIDI/note data
3. **Generate** a cover that matches YOUR melody, YOUR phrasing, YOUR key
4. **Reconstruct** the song with new instrumentation around your vocal performance
5. Or, if you prefer: use your melody as a guide and create entirely new vocal renditions
